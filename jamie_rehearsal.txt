JAMIE: It’s a vendor-reported customer story, so that’s Microsoft’s claim, backed by customer testimony, not an independent benchmark. And the measurement context is specific: data-gathering time only. No published controlled study, no revenue uplift, no universal percentage.
RUFUS: So the limitation is: quick answer doesn’t mean correct answer. Excellent. Even AI needs receipts.
JAMIE: Exactly. Fast retrieval does not establish that an insight is correct or that a product will sell. You still validate the cited source. ConsumerIQ is for internal company use anyway, not a public app, so this is about making search less painful inside the organization.
ALEX: For a normal person, what’s the move? Because I don’t have an internal beauty empire in my pocket.
JAMIE: The small first step is delightfully boring: choose a handful of approved research documents, ask one repeatable question, inspect the citations, and time both speed and accuracy. RUFUS: I was going to say “lipstick on a spreadsheet,” but your version is sharper.
JAMIE: Thank you. The payoff is practical: less hunting, more evidence, and a human still making the call. That’s the transformation—research from weeks to minutes, with the proof trail still attached.
